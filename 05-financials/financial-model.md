## 1. Business case

_Why this initiative is worth funding over the alternatives. Include the key unit economics assumptions, CAC, LTV, payback period, where relevant._

**Rock #1: AI-Generated Daily Check-In Prompts** (Product) — chosen because it is the direct implementation of the "capabilities required" row in the M1 OKR cascade and the mechanism behind KR1 and KR3.

*Figures marked **[assumed]** are illustrative placeholders — replace with real numbers before this goes in front of an actual CFO.*

**The strategic bet:** Personalizing daily check-in prompts to a user's own history and journey stage (acute vs. post-acute) will extend engagement past the 90–120 day point where users currently resolve their acute need and quietly stop opening the app. It serves existing users, 25–45, who have already passed acute-need resolution. The mechanism: retained users (1) avoid a re-acquisition cost, and (2) build the addressable base for the premium personalization tier already committed as the path forward on the therapist-matching hard no.

**The assumptions, ranked (most consequential if wrong, first):**

1. That personalized *content style alone* (not new content, not new mechanics) is sufficient to hold attention post-acute — the single riskiest assumption, and the one the kill criterion under-protects (see below).
2. That the analytics engine can accurately detect a user's journey phase.
3. That this is buildable in-house within the 0–3 month Now window without pulling capacity from Rocks #2/#3.
4. That retained users will later pay for deeper personalization (second-order — matters to the premium tier, not this quarter's return).

**Scale assumptions:** **[assumed]** 50,000 current weekly-active users; **[assumed]** ~10,000 fall in the day 91–120 cohort per quarter.

**The expected return:**
- **Unit level:** $38 avoided CAC per retained user, before any premium-tier upside.
- **At scale, kill-threshold case** (+5pts, 15%→20%): 500 incremental retained users × $38 = **$19,000**/quarter in avoided CAC — well below the $180,000 build cost.
- **At scale, full KR1 target** (+15pts, 15%→30%): 1,500 incremental retained users × $38 = **$57,000**/quarter — still below build cost, implying payback on CAC-avoidance alone would take roughly 3+ quarters even at full target.

> **The case in one paragraph:** CAC-avoidance alone does not justify this investment on its own economics — at even the full KR1 target, avoided-CAC return stays below the assumed build cost. The real financial case rests on unlocking the premium tier once retention is proven: this Rock is a prerequisite bet, not a standalone ROI-positive one, and should be presented to leadership as such rather than implied.

## 2. Kill criteria

_The specific signals that would tell you this bet is no longer worth pursuing. Be explicit about the metric, the threshold, and the timeline._

> If **90-day+ retention (KR1)** does not reach **20% (a 5-point lift from the 15% baseline)** by **end of Q2 2026**, the **[assumed] $180,000 Q1 investment is treated as a loss against a failed retention lift**, remaining engineering capacity is **reallocated to Rocks #2/#3 for the rest of the year**, and the **premium-tier plan already committed to the CEO is deferred by at least two quarters**.
